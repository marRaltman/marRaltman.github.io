what i'm still working out

I don't usually write about this part directly, and I'm not going to here either — not because it's a secret, but because some things need more room than a bio page can hold. What I will say is that a few years ago I went through something that genuinely knocked me down, and that the version of me writing this right now exists because I did the unglamorous, repetitive work of building myself back. New support system. New habits. A hard-won, still-fragile sense that the ground under me is actually solid.

That rebuilding was the biggest project of my life so far, and for a long time, safety was the whole goal. Not comfort — safety. The kind you have to prove to your own nervous system, over and over, before it believes you. I got there. I'm there now, mostly, on most days. And I assumed that once I arrived, I'd just get to rest in it.

Instead I've run into a different problem: the same person who spent years needing all that stability is the one who now can't stop looking at the door. I want to travel further than feels reasonable. I want to test what I'm actually capable of, somewhere nobody already knows my name or my history. I want — and I say this a little embarrassed — to be extraordinary. Not performatively, not for an audience. Just because I think there's more in me than I've used yet, and I'd like to find out before I run out of time to try.

The wanting and the safety don't cancel each other out, logically. I know that. You're supposed to build the stable thing so you have somewhere to launch from, not so you never leave. But some weeks it doesn't feel that clean. Some weeks it feels like I'm going to spend the rest of my life negotiating between the part of me that wants to stay very still and very safe, and the part of me that wants to get on a plane alone and see what's on the other side of being scared. I genuinely don't know which one wins most days. I'm not sure I'm supposed to know yet.

"Maybe the goal was never to stop wanting more. Maybe it was just to get safe enough to survive wanting it." — something I keep circling back to.

So this page isn't really about a place. It's an honest note to myself, left somewhere I'll actually see it again: I haven't figured out how to fully hold both the calling to be extraordinary and the need to feel safe, and I might not for a long time. I'm choosing to reach anyway — a little unsteadily, on purpose — and see what happens.

And I think that's allowed.
