# Problem

## The short version

People who like to make things have two problems. Their big projects stall halfway because nothing keeps track of supplies, cost, and time. And between big projects, they don't make anything at all, because starting from a blank page is hard and life is busy.

## Who has this problem

- **Hobby crafters** who paint, sew, knit, build, or upcycle, and have a pile of unfinished projects.
- **DIYers** working on home, furniture, or gift projects with a deadline.
- **Students and busy people** who used to be creative and want to get back into it in small amounts.
- **Beginners** who want to try something new but don't know where to start.

## What goes wrong

### With big projects

1. **No clear supply list.** People buy things they already own or get stuck mid-project missing one piece.
2. **Time is a guess.** Steps take longer than expected, and waiting time (paint, glue, stain, dye drying) gets forgotten.
3. **Costs creep.** Small purchases add up without anyone noticing.
4. **Too many projects at once.** The one with a deadline gets pushed aside by whichever is most fun.

### With everyday creativity

5. **Blank-page freeze.** With no prompt, it's easier not to start at all.
6. **All-or-nothing thinking.** If there isn't time for a big project, people skip making anything.
7. **Wrong difficulty.** Tutorials are often too easy to be interesting or too hard to finish, so people quit.

## Why it matters

Making things is good for people, not just a way to fill time. When projects stall and creative habits fade, people lose something that helps them relax and feel capable. Half-finished projects also waste money and materials.

## Problem statement

> People who enjoy crafting and DIY can't keep their big projects organized or keep a regular creative habit going, so projects stall, money and materials get wasted, and they make less than they want to.
